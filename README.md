# DrawBreath

**Knowing When to Do Less: Adaptive AI Withdrawal in Children's Creative Drawing**

[Paper](assets/paper/drawbreath-paper.pdf) · [Code, Data & Study Materials](https://github.com/Draw-Breath/DrawBreath_Supplementary-Material)

AI can support children's creative work, but its continued presence may outlast the need for help. **DrawBreath** uses canvas activity and interaction history to determine when to **Stay**, **Observe**, or **Withdraw**, with interruptible fading and child-initiated re-entry.

In a single-condition study, **65 sixth-grade children** completed an open-ended drawing continuation task. Analysis combined participation judgments, interaction logs, and post-task evaluations. Stable drawing followed **80% of completed withdrawals**, and children generally reported being able to continue drawing afterward, suggesting that withdrawal often occurred at moments compatible with continued independent creation.

Illustrative trajectories situated withdrawal within ongoing elaboration, revision, and later reorientation, sometimes followed by renewed help-seeking. The findings frame withdrawal as a **reversible adjustment within creation**, informing the design of AI support that can step back while remaining accessible. They do not establish a uniquely optimal withdrawal time.
